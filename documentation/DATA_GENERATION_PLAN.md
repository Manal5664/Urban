## Fully generated input and provenance strategy

The current **PROJECT DESIGN CHOICE** is a fully generated, deterministic and reproducible competition dataset. The official SRS requires teams to create their own large-scale interconnected dataset and submit generation scripts, and says that a ready-made dataset without significant transformation, integration and augmentation is insufficient; it does **not** require a hybrid strategy, a public/Kaggle source, or any particular external provider. This design therefore creates the fictional topology, calendars, operations, passengers, demand, observations and controlled defects from original generator logic. No external data is selected, downloaded or required in this phase.

The generated source channels are explicitly transformed, integrated and augmented through project logic: coordinate/time/ID conventions are canonicalized; route patterns, stop sequences, calendars, supply, demand, transactions, movement, count, delay, GPS and context channels are joined through lineage and keys; and seasonal variation, disruptions, replacements, difficult conditions and quality defects are added. Because the SRS does not define how its warning applies to wholly synthetic data, this is a conservative interpretation to document and validate, not a claim that the SRS mandates external data. A future external source would require a separate reviewed decision, license/provenance record and proof of significant transformation, integration and augmentation; it cannot be assumed or used to satisfy a minimum by itself. The reproducibility contract is the versioned generator configuration, master seed, identity namespace, canonical serialization, source/config hashes and dataset manifest.

Travel/dwell/demand must be simulated together. A high boarding load can increase dwell, causing late arrival and bunching; that changes subsequent queues and served/unserved outcomes. Calibrate generator configuration using structural targets and a designated pilot/training subset, never by choosing final-test data that make models look accurate. Publish limitations of a simulated network. Iterative pilot/calibration is a future phase, not permission to generate even sample records in this phase.

Determinism: master seed + versioned scenario/entity/date substreams; stable business keys; independent random streams for topology, supply, demand, operational events and raw corruption. Raw duplicate copies preserve the original business key but get a different physical source_row_id. Changing the raw fault rate must not reshuffle all clean-world operations. Shard count/parallelism must not change entity IDs, latent demand or business records. Save configuration and source hashes, not just a seed. Future generation writes immutable table/date shards incrementally and may generate demand/observations in deterministic chunks; it must not require one in-memory dataset. Later Spark processing reads partitioned raw files, and the Python path uses chunked/streaming or spilled joins/aggregates; neither path may assume that concatenating every chunk into one Pandas dataframe is safe.

## Safety budgets and acceptance counts

Baseline **2,400,000** unique served rides precedes corruption. Append-only duplicate ticket copies (about 0.5%, approximately 12,000) do not increase usable targets. The main raw dataset may contain omissions and invalid values; protected reference examples sufficient for all minimums are never destructively mutated. Keep the private canonical simulation solely as a test oracle, not an input to cleaning that would make reconstruction trivial.

Per table, the **union** of destructive direct corruptions (including missing records) is capped at **2%** of baseline physical business rows; overlapping injections are counted once. Total loss after transitive FK/temporal/join exclusions is capped at **4%** for Tickets, Passenger_Journeys, Passenger_Counts and Delays. This implies baseline floors of **2,304,000 rides, 2,257,920 count observations and 288,000 positive delays**, above the project publication targets of 2,300,000, 2,250,000 and 280,000 respectively and above the SRS minima. Corrections/valid flags can reduce actual loss, but the plan does not depend on optimistic repair rates.

Dimension damages are confined to challenge copies or bounded subsets whose dependent loss fits the same budget. Maintain valid referenced coverage of ≥110 routes, ≥600 used stops, ≥300 actually assigned vehicles, ≥75,000 used passengers, all **18** history months and multiple calendars/schedules. Do not delete an entire busy route or schedule to inject a single missing-ID example. Standalone challenge fixtures can intentionally exceed rates but are reported separately, never mixed into production statistics or used to inflate minimum counts. Each final count is measured on accepted usable records, with distinct-ID and nonzero-count breakdowns. Target failure blocks publication and requires an explicit new generator version/configuration, not silent arbitrary filling or suppressed audit issues.

## Canonical movement and no-double-counting rule

`Passenger_Journeys` is the canonical passenger-movement view. `Tickets` is a separate payment/ticketing view of the same baseline ride. Report distinct usable journeys, distinct usable tickets and their overlap, but count the SRS movement minimum once using the greater named view; never add the two tables. A confirmed journey with a missing ticket remains movement evidence, while a ticket without a journey is not silently promoted to a second movement. Duplicate physical ticket copies, requests, stop counts, GPS points, transfer events, superseded plan rows and partitions are not additional movements. The SRS does not define the precise overlap/grain, so this project convention remains visible for evaluator confirmation.

## Sixteen mandatory data-quality rule families

Each rule has versioned detection, evidence, severity, permitted correction, final status and a future acceptance test. Rates are project injection choices, **not SRS requirements**. Severe unknown/invalid references affect ≤0.05–0.10% of relevant fact rows per family, subject to the 2% union / 4% dependent-loss budget. Isolated malformed dimension examples use challenge copies to avoid unbounded cascade.

| Rule / SRS issue | Detection / cross-record evidence | Planned injection and rate | Cleaning/flagging/quarantine and later acceptance test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DQ01 Missing ticket records | Journey ticket_id null or reference absent in Tickets; movement channel still establishes ride | Omit ≤0.10% ticket rows outside protected cohort; distinguish absent transaction from invalid ticket key | Preserve known journey as MISSING_TICKET with nullable canonical FK and audit original reference; exclude sale counts, never fabricate ticket; test missing versus valid zero-fare ticket |
| DQ02 Missing route IDs | Null route_id in Trips/Route_Stops or unresolved route reference; pattern/schedule may corroborate unique intended parent | Null/break ≤0.05% relevant fact references | Restore only when immutable referenced pattern unambiguously supplies same route; otherwise quarantine affected relationship; test conflicting versus unambiguous parent |
| DQ03 Invalid stop IDs | No Stops parent, or referenced Route_Stop disagrees with endpoint/stop-event location | Alter ≤0.05% ticket/journey endpoints or bounded route-stop copies | Repair only using valid same-visit route_stop metadata; unresolved wrong stop quarantined for OD; test known stop on wrong pattern too |
| DQ04 Duplicate ticket transactions | Duplicate ticket_id or transaction_ref; compare exact content and source revision provenance | C02 0.5% exact and ≤0.05% conflicting appended copies | Exact duplicate survivor by stable source priority + ordinal; others DUPLICATE_REMOVED; conflicting groups quarantine unless authoritative revision evidence; test dedup independent of file order |
| DQ05 Duplicate trips | Repeated trip_id or same `(schedule_id,service_date,instance_index)` | Append duplicates for ~0.10% trip rows; subset conflicting ≤0.02% | Canonical exact survivor; avoid counting/rejoining copies; conflicts quarantine affected trip facts within loss budget; test valid extra departure with different instance index |
| DQ06 Negative passenger counts | Any boardings/alightings/onboard <0; also failed load conservation | Corrupt ≤0.10% count records | Recompute from independently available accepted journeys/events only if complete coverage and evidence; otherwise quarantine count observation, do not clip to zero; test remaining overload signal |
| DQ07 Invalid timestamps | Lexical parse, missing timezone under unknown source, impossible calendar values | C03 ≤0.10% relevant rows | Parse only documented unambiguous alternate format; ambiguous/missing critical timestamp quarantined; preserve original text; test invalid calendar date and offset |
| DQ08 Impossible arrival times | Next arrival before prior departure, before feasible travel-time bound, inconsistent trip/date or gross GPS speed discontinuity | Mutate ≤0.05% observed stop times, leave genuine early service unchanged | Correct only from corroborating signed/source event evidence; else quarantine temporal observation, propagate usability to derived duration/labels; test early but physically valid vs impossible |
| DQ09 Departure before arrival | Same-stop actual departure <actual arrival | Swap/corrupt ≤0.05% stop events | No blind swap: require source evidence; else quarantine event timing and dependent time labels while retaining separately valid count evidence if usable; test cancellation nulls excluded |
| DQ10 Vehicle capacity violations | Observed load >effective actual capacity; evaluate conservation, independent journeys and assignment range | C13 real 3–5% overloaded trips; add ≤0.05% count rows with absurd sensor spikes | Real overload FLAGGED, retained for crowding; wrong capacity corrected only from historical assignment evidence; impossible unexplained measurement quarantined. Never cap all loads to capacity; test both paths |
| DQ11 Invalid delay values | Negative in positive-delay table, both components zero, or mismatch with actual−scheduled positive differences | Mutate ≤0.10% delay rows incl negative/mismatched values | Recompute from accepted same-stop times; otherwise quarantine delay only; do not remove genuine long delay solely for magnitude; test early arrival belongs outside positive-delay table |
| DQ12 Missing vehicle assignments | Required planned vehicle missing or operated stop lacks covering ACTUAL assignment; gaps/overlaps | Null/omit ≤0.05% planned/actual assignment references, bounded dependent rows | Restore only from unique trusted actual assignment/GPS reference; never assume planned vehicle was actual after substitution; quarantine capacity-risk labels if unknown; test cancelled trip legitimately has no ACTUAL segment |
| DQ13 Broken stop sequences | Duplicate/gapped sequence, wrong pattern, nonmonotonic cumulative distance, actual visit ordering inconsistent with status | Corrupt ≤0.05% route-stop challenge rows or observed references | Correct from immutable schedule/pattern copy with authority; otherwise quarantine broken pattern references; do not confuse repeated physical stop loop with duplicate sequence; test legitimate skip with explicit SKIPPED event |
| DQ14 Invalid route distances | Zero/negative length, cumulative distance contradiction, path shorter than plausible coordinate bound | Corrupt ≤0.05% pattern/route-stop challenge rows with capped dependency impact | Recalculate from valid detailed geometry only; do not silently substitute straight-line distance for unknown actual path; unresolved distance excluded from load-factor/travel-distance tasks; test temporal version preserved |
| DQ15 Unknown passengers | Ticket/journey/request passenger_id lacks valid parent or predates registration | Replace ≤0.05% fact IDs; hidden challenge can be higher | Accept new passenger only with valid supplied metadata; otherwise quarantine identity-dependent records; do not synthesize real attributes or silently drop from report; test late dimension arrival reprocessing |
| DQ16 Missing trip records | Ticket/journey/count/GPS references absent trip; planned departure with no observation separately classified | Omit ≤0.02% parent trip rows in bounded nonprotected cohort or break ≤0.05% references | Recover only if complete authoritative plan instance establishes identity; otherwise quarantine unresolved dependents; do not fabricate completed trip or conflate cancelled trip with missing record; test both |

Additional checks cover PK completeness/uniqueness, FK/date validity, unknown vehicles, trip/assignment coherence, capacity positivity, fare domain, coordinate bounds, passenger/vehicle overlapping journeys/duties, count conservation, JSON/CSV parse failures, file/schema drift, negative travel time, missing expected delays, duplicate GPS timestamps and out-of-order ingestion. Unknown vehicles are explicit hidden-data tests even though Step 4 phrases the assignment case differently. No automatic imputation of route IDs, stop IDs, future timestamps or outcomes from a statistical model is planned.

## Audit execution contract

Immutable raw is the evidence of what arrived, including duplicates and malformed values. Never overwrite it or physically delete its rows during cleaning. “Remove” means remove from accepted analytical outputs while preserving original source and final audit disposition. For records omitted during controlled injection, the oracle records the omission; runtime detection relies on surviving references/source reconciliation, not oracle access.

For each independent processing run:

1. Create raw envelope with file path, physical ordinal, row hash and stable source_row_id; capture parse failures with original bytes/text.
2. Evaluate applicable rule versions against accessible source data. Record one DQ_Issues row per rule/field issue with original record/value, issue, cleaning rule, evidence, proposed correction and action.
3. Apply corrections only with justified evidence and deterministic precedence: parse/schema/identity → FK/temporal consistency → count/delay consistency → analytical flags. Unresolved blocking errors override accepted/flagged status; exact duplicate removal points to a survivor. Multiple issues are not multiple removed records.
4. Finalize one DQ_Record_Outcomes entry per **affected** raw row: `ACCEPTED_CORRECTED`, `ACCEPTED_FLAGGED`, `QUARANTINED`, `REMOVED`, `DUPLICATE_REMOVED` or `OTHER_DOCUMENTED`; corrected record/value is nullable when no correction. Unchanged valid rows are counted at run/table level and do not receive an audit outcome. Link all issue IDs and task-specific usability. A FLAGGED overload remains usable for risk modelling; a missing ticket need not erase a confirmed movement.
5. Reconcile every raw row with the explicit equation `raw = accepted unchanged + accepted corrected + accepted flagged + quarantined + removed/deduplicated + other documented dispositions`. Reconcile canonical accepted PKs, duplicates, joins and task-specific denominators separately. Report every excluded row and its dependency cascade. A late-arriving parent leads to a new versioned run/result, not silent mutation of prior evidence.
6. Generate Data Quality Report by table/rule/month/severity/status, before/after null/duplicate/FK counts, original/corrected examples, correction rationale, lost usable records, residual flags, affected analytical tasks, and minimum/target gates. Preserve machine-readable counts, a human-readable report, run/config/schema hashes and processing logs.

**Revised audit planning volume:** the target has approximately 13.5 million physical raw business rows including duplicate ticket copies and the replacement-transfer ledger. With the bounded direct/dependent defect budgets, plan for roughly 100,000–400,000 `DQ_Issues` and 80,000–300,000 affected `DQ_Record_Outcomes` per pipeline/run: about 180,000–700,000 audit rows per pipeline or 360,000–1,400,000 across both pipelines. Unchanged valid rows have no individual audit record; they are covered by run/table/file reconciliation. These are estimates, not caps, and the measured report must replace them.

The generator's private ground truth and injection catalog are test oracles only. Detectors cannot “correct” by looking up pristine generator output, and models cannot use injection flags. Measure detection precision/recall against the oracle after results are frozen; residual ambiguity is reported. No measured quality or model metric is claimed in this plan.

## Temporal/split and hidden-data acceptance

Use the 18-month history and 12/3/3-month chronological split from architecture. Availability and event time are both checked, and every case records `prediction_cutoff` (normally its issue time). Purge examples whose forecast label horizon straddles boundaries; group all representations/duplicates of a trip into one partition. Features/thresholds/encoders must be fitted using only permitted history. Null unavailable future actual values rather than filling them with scheduled values and pretending they are observations. A known future schedule is valid only when its publication precedes issue time.

## Independent pipelines and later ML acceptance

Pipeline A later reads the shared immutable raw snapshot through HDFS/Spark/PySpark, performs its own validation, joins, features, preprocessing and Spark MLlib fitting. Pipeline B independently reads the equivalent raw snapshot with chunked Pandas/NumPy and suitable Python DS libraries, then performs its own validation, joins, features, preprocessing and model fitting. They may share source bytes, identifiers, schema semantics, split membership and case truth; they must not share predictions, fitted models, fitted preprocessing artifacts or derived model outputs. Do not duplicate unrelated processing merely to appear independent, and do not use Spark-cleaned data as the Python pipeline's input.

The later evaluation target remains the SRS criterion of **at least 85% test accuracy OR macro-F1 >=0.80 where appropriate** for classification, and **measurable improvement over a documented simple baseline** for forecasting. These are future acceptance criteria, not measured results. Dataset design must support delay prediction/severity, route clustering, demand forecasting, occupancy/crowding risk, route/stop performance, anomaly detection, frequency recommendations and what-if analysis without future leakage; no model performance is fabricated here.

validators now exist under `data_generator/` and `sample_data/smoke/`.  The
delivery statuses below remain conservative: the smoke fixtures do not replace
independent cleaning, production-scale measurements, Parquet/HDFS artifacts, or
model evidence.

Related Spark evidence (§1.10(4)): HDFS ingestion, ingestion scripts, schemas, cleaning transformations, joins, Spark SQL queries, aggregations, partition strategy, Parquet output, logs and performance results. All are future outputs; a path or prose plan is not execution evidence. Later tests include functional/integration, ingestion/schema/quality, Spark/SQL, model/forecast/delay/occupancy/route/stop, dual-pipeline/anomaly/hidden-data/security/boundary categories (§1.10(9)). Security/privacy design uses synthetic identities, source-license review, restricted oracle access, non-secret manifests and role-aware future data access; exact applicable regulation remains to be assessed.

Minimum/target acceptance tests will independently verify each of the nine scale rows; only unique accepted usable records count. Reproducibility tests vary chunking/order/workers while preserving business records and IDs. Relationship tests validate each expected join with known cardinalities and deliberately broken references. Temporal tests prove no future event/availability, label-window or group leakage. Analytics tests reconcile boarding−alighting=load change, passenger-km/capacity-km, request→journey waiting, stop-level delay differences, headway and peak detection under shifted demand. Future pilot results must demonstrate nontrivial variability without impossible vehicle/passenger trajectories; passing documentation review does not establish any performance or ML accuracy claim.

## Explicit ambiguity and gap disposition

- **Trip-level passenger records:** no grain is defined. The project target is 2.4 million `Passenger_Journeys` passenger–trip rows; `Passenger_Counts` separately has about 2.352 million stop observations with a project gate of ≥500,000 nonzero observations. The design reports both grains and does not call either one 500,000 distinct trips. Confirm the evaluator's accepted grain before final generation; if a distinct-trip interpretation is required, scale only through an approved configuration change and recheck VM resources.
- **Counting after cleaning/overlap/history:** the SRS does not specify raw versus usable count, whether the two movement/passenger minima overlap, or calendar-month boundary convention. This design measures each separately after cleaning, reports the ticket/journey overlap, retains 18 full months and never adds duplicate representations to inflate size. No confirmation is assumed.
- **Multiple calendars/schedules and illustrative lists:** no numeric calendar count is supplied and “such as” does not make each example independently mandatory. Project targets 12 calendars/about 2,600 schedule versions with a ≥2,400 usable floor and explicitly elects all twelve entities/all 21 conditions. Table physical names and specific anomaly rates are design choices.
- **Required joins SHOULD / features “such as”:** all ten joins and all 24 feature examples are supported despite mixed wording. Source text and modality are retained in the register.
- **Mislabelled Step 12 and anomaly wording:** plan overcrowding, not audio monitoring; §1.6 says “Normal” behavior while Step 39 says unusual behavior. Preserve both in master and request correction, no invented sound hardware.
- **MAY versus mandatory functional capability:** delay severity and segmentation are supported; category names/methods are not forced into SRS minima. The §1.7 blanket MUST interpretation remains as documented in master.
- **Undefined thresholds/targets:** no official defect percentages, occupancy bands, persistent-window sizes, stop-utilization denominator, route-score weights, forecast horizon or baseline margin is supplied. Proposed formulas and generator ranges are labelled project choices; operational thresholds remain future documented configuration. Do not use sample recommendation numbers as constants.
- **Hidden-data schema:** fixed identifiers and novel categories are supported, but unknown file/schema contracts need an adapter/missing-field decision after evaluator input. This is not waived by synthetic generation.
- **Public input and privacy:** the SRS does not prescribe an external source or hybrid strategy. This project uses fully generated synthetic channels, so there is no external-source selection blocker. Any future public input would require license/provenance review; regulatory applicability remains to be assessed. No real passenger identifiers are planned.
- **Storage and resources:** explicit HDFS/CSV/JSON/Parquet requirements are preserved despite software-list alternatives. The 2.4 million-ride / 450,000-GPS target is a resource-safe project choice for the stated VM, not an SRS minimum. The later pilot must measure memory/disk/time and the compact audit volume; no throughput or disk-fit claim is made here.
- **Annual seasonality:** the planned 18-month operational history satisfies 12 months, and the 12-month training segment contains one complete annual cycle. It is still not proof of stable multi-year seasonality; use documented seasonal-naive fallbacks based on available history and do not invent annual evidence.
